# Question 6 — AI Literacy: Prompt Engineering

**Format:** Short Answer

---

Read both prompts below carefully, then answer the questions that follow.

**Prompt A**

> I am a marketing manager at a retail company and we have just finished a three-month campaign. My team has collected customer feedback through an online survey and we now have about 500 responses stored in a spreadsheet. Each response includes the customer's age group, the product they purchased, their satisfaction rating from 1 to 5, and a short written comment. I need to present the findings to our CEO next Friday in a way that is easy to understand. Can you analyse this data for me, highlight which age groups and products have the lowest satisfaction scores, identify the most common complaints from the written comments, and summarise everything in a short paragraph I can use as an executive summary?

**Prompt B**

> **Role:** You are a data analyst helping a retail marketing team.  
> **Task:** Analyse customer survey data from a 3-month campaign.  
> **Data:** 500 responses containing age group, product purchased, satisfaction rating (1-5), and written comments.  
> **Steps:**
> 1. Identify age groups and products with the lowest satisfaction scores.
> 2. Extract the most common themes from the written comments.
> 3. Summarise findings in an executive summary paragraph.
>
> **Audience:** CEO presentation on Friday.  
> **Constraints:** Keep the summary concise and free of technical jargon.

---

## Task 1 — Analysis

**(a)** Which prompt do you think will get a better response from an AI? State your choice (A or B).

**Answer:**  
**B**

---

**(b)** Give **two reasons** to support your choice.

**Reason 1:**  
Prompt B is structured into clearly labelled parts (Role, Task, Data, Steps, Audience, Constraints), so the AI can easily see exactly what is being asked and is less likely to miss or misread a requirement buried in a long paragraph.

**Reason 2:**  
Prompt B breaks the work into numbered steps and adds an explicit constraint (concise, no technical jargon), which tells the AI both the order of the analysis and the style of the final output, so the answer is more consistent and easier to check against the request.

---

**(c)** What is **one strength** of the prompt you did NOT choose?

**Answer:**  
Prompt A gives richer context about the situation and purpose: who is asking (a marketing manager), why (presenting to the CEO next Friday) and what the end goal is (an easy-to-understand executive summary), which helps the AI tailor its tone and judge what really matters to the reader.

---

## Task 2 — Rewrite

Rewrite either prompt by borrowing **one element** from the other to make it stronger. Then explain what you borrowed and why.

**Your rewritten prompt:**

> **Role:** You are a data analyst helping a retail marketing team.  
> **Context:** I am a marketing manager. We have just finished a 3-month campaign and I need to present the results to our CEO next Friday. The CEO is not technical and wants to know quickly where customers were least satisfied and why.  
> **Task:** Analyse the customer survey data from the campaign.  
> **Data:** 500 responses in a spreadsheet, each containing age group, product purchased, satisfaction rating (1-5), and a short written comment.  
> **Steps:**
> 1. Identify the age groups and products with the lowest average satisfaction scores.
> 2. Extract the most common complaint themes from the written comments.
> 3. Summarise the findings in one executive summary paragraph.
>
> **Audience:** CEO presentation on Friday.  
> **Constraints:** Keep the summary concise (under 150 words) and free of technical jargon.

**What you borrowed and why:**  
I kept Prompt B's structure and borrowed Prompt A's **context**: who I am, why I need the analysis, and what the CEO cares about. Prompt B says *what* to do but not *why*; adding the purpose and the reader helps the AI decide which findings to emphasise and how to phrase them for a non-technical CEO, while the labelled structure still keeps the request clear. I also made "concise" measurable (under 150 words) so the output is easier to check.
